1

Your gut takes more of a beating than the average person's. That part isn't in your head.

Here's what no one selling greens powder will admit: the cleaner your diet gets, the harder it can be on your stomach.

Look at your day. A scoop or two of whey, a pre-workout, a low-carb bar in the afternoon. Spotless on paper. But every one of them is working against your gut.

Whey is packed with lactose, the milk sugar most adults can't fully break down. One shake, maybe you handle it. Three a day, every day, and your gut is drowning in more than it can clear. It ferments into gas, and that gas is the bloat that hits an hour after you drink it.

Then there's everything sweetened with zero calories. The sucralose in your pre-workout, the sugar alcohols in the bar. Your body can't absorb them, so they sit in your gut, pull in water, and ferment there too. That's the sudden swelling after a snack you swore was fine.

None of this is your fault. The exact foods you use to build your body punish your gut harder than junk food would. So a general wellness pill made for someone eating salads was never going to fix it.

2

Most of the probiotics you tried were dead before they reached your gut.

This is the big one, and it's why "I tried three brands and nothing changed" is the most common sentence in this whole category.

Here's what happens when you swallow a normal probiotic. The capsule hits your stomach and dissolves in minutes, then splits open and dumps its bacteria straight into acid strong enough to kill almost anything. So it does. Most of them die on the spot, long before they reach the gut where they were supposed to work.

What survives is a tiny scrap of what you paid for.

Picture dropping people into a pool of acid in a paper boat. The boat melts, and they're gone before they reach the other side. That's a normal capsule.

What you want instead is a sealed submarine, something the acid can't get into, that stays shut through your stomach and only opens once it reaches the calm part of your gut.

Whether the bacteria arrive alive or dead matters more than anything else printed on the label. And it's the exact corner most brands cut, because a real shell costs money to build. So they skip it, print a big number on the front, and count on you not noticing that the whole thing died on the way down.

A longer list of strains doesn't make it work better.

Once you know to look, you'll see brands brag about strain count like it's a trophy. Ten strains, fifteen, some push past twenty.

For your gut, that's backwards.

A longer list isn't a stronger formula, it's a bigger number for the label. Your gut has limited room, and when you flood it with a dozen different bacteria at once, they fight for space instead of lining up politely. A gut already irritated by everything in point one gets buried under far more than it can handle.

That's where the rough starts come from. Someone begins a big multi-strain pill, feels worse for a week or two with more bloating, cramping, and loose stool, and the brand calls it an "adjustment period" and tells them to push through.

That's not your body healing. That's your body overwhelmed. You got handed a crowd when what you needed was a small team. And if you want to look good in a gym set this weekend, being told to suffer for two weeks first is a terrible trade.

A bigger "billions" number doesn't make it work better either.

The other number they love is CFU. You've seen it climb: 40 billion, 50 billion, 100 billion, higher and higher like it's a contest.

Go back to point two. If the delivery is bad, most of that count dies in your stomach anyway, so a capsule bragging about 50 billion that arrives mostly dead loses to a smaller, protected dose that arrives alive and ready. The big number is measuring the wrong thing.

All it really tells you is how much they crammed in before it got destroyed. That's not how well it works. That's how good their marketing is.
